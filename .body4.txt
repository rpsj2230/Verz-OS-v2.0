A passage knows how it was read, page by page, and a scan that was not clear says which pages were left out

Stacked on the embedding part. The owner's requirement (GAP3-04 in the register) is that a
citation can tell the reader how the passage it cites was read, **per page when one document was
read more than one way**. This is the stored half: every block, passage and chunk row carries the
path and engine that read it, a PDF whose pages are partly photographed is read two ways and says so
page by page, and the pages of a scan that were not clear enough are named on the item and to the
uploader. The citation that SHOWS it is not here. Leaves touched and none closed: M7.7.9, M7.7.10.

What it adds, and why it is shaped this way

* `Block`, `Passage` and `Chunk` gain `path` and `engine`, both or neither. They are set by the
  route that read the block and never by the reader's answer: the decoder builds a block from a
  closed list of keys and these are not on it, and a test reads an answer carrying them and shows
  they are dropped. They are closed to three words and the bounded engine alphabet at construction,
  so they cannot carry a sentence into a column that is shown. A passage is cut from one block and
  never spans two, so a passage's path is its block's and a document read two ways has passages of
  each.
* `know.chunk.parse_path` and `parse_engine` (migration 0250, already in the frame) are written for
  every row, NULL together for a passage read before reading was recorded; the table's own
  constraints (one of three words, both or neither) are exercised against a real PostgreSQL.
* A layout read of a PDF leaves a photographed page blank, silently, in a document that reads as
  indexed. So the pages whose text layer is empty and which the layout read produced no block on are
  sent to the scanned-page part, which reads only those (`pages` on the request, an optional key
  both programs spell and a test holds equal), and its blocks are laid in among the layout's by
  page, each saying OCR. An outage there fails the whole read retryably rather than indexing
  without the pages it could not read yet; a refusal of another kind leaves the pages out and names
  them.
* A page under the floor is named on the item (`know.item.parse_notice`, in the uploader's words
  and page numbers only, within its column) and on the ticket, which then reads "was added. Page 2
  was not clear enough to read reliably..." rather than "was added".
* Chunk rows refresh the two columns on a re-ingest, so a document re-read without a route is
  NULL again and never keeps a path it no longer has.

What was rejected

* A path per document: the requirement says per page, and a document-level path is a lie for the
  pages that were filled in.
* The reader reporting its own path: a server that grew the key could record a guess as a reading.
* Reading every page of a layout-read PDF with OCR "to be safe": minutes, and it replaces text that
  was read with text that was guessed.

Not done, and the owner should know: **no screen or citation shows how a passage was read yet.**
Doing it means carrying the column through retrieval, the field policy that decides what a reader is
shown, `DocumentCitation` and its rendering: a change to the surface that decides what a reader may
be told, which wants its own pull request and its own review. M7.7.9 stays open on that.

Reused: knowledge.chunking (unchanged cutting; gains the two fields), chunk_store (`chunk_row`,
`upsert_statement`), parse_paths (`ParseProvenance` and its bounded engine alphabet),
parse_ocr (`read_by_page`, `left_out_notice`), text_path (`laid_out`, the PDF reader), item_store.
Replaced: nothing.
